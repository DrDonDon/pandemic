# Conjecture C1 — Continuity is necessary and sufficient for CTRW convergence in spatially varying anomalous diffusion

**ID:** C1  
**Status:** open  
**Precision:** formalised  

---

## Motivation

In spatially varying anomalous diffusion, the diffusion exponents $\alpha(x)$ (space-fractional)
and $\beta(x)$ (time-fractional) vary with position. The microscopic model is a Continuous Time
Random Walk (CTRW): at position $x$, the particle waits a random time with tail $\sim t^{-1-\beta(x)}$
then jumps a random distance with tail $\sim |\xi|^{-1-\alpha(x)}$.

The macroscopic description should be a variable-order fractional diffusion PDE. The question is:
under what regularity conditions on $\alpha(x)$ and $\beta(x)$ does the CTRW scaling limit exist
and converge to this PDE?

Background with Angstman group (UNSW); context is CTRW / fractional Fokker-Planck.

---

## Formal Statement

**Setup.** Let $\alpha, \beta: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be measurable functions satisfying
$$0 < \alpha_{\min} \leq \alpha(x) \leq \alpha_{\max} < 2, \qquad 0 < \beta_{\min} \leq \beta(x) \leq \beta_{\max} < 1$$
for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$.

For each $\epsilon > 0$, let $\{X^\epsilon(t)\}_{t \geq 0}$ denote the rescaled CTRW in which:
- at position $x$, the waiting time distribution has tail $P(T > t; x) = L_\beta(x, t) \cdot t^{-\beta(x)}$
  where $L_\beta(x, \cdot)$ is slowly varying uniformly in $x$,
- at position $x$, the jump distribution has tail $P(|\xi| > r; x) = L_\alpha(x, r) \cdot r^{-\alpha(x)}$
  where $L_\alpha(x, \cdot)$ is slowly varying uniformly in $x$,
- and $X^\epsilon$ is obtained by scaling space by $\epsilon^{1/\bar\alpha}$ and time by $\epsilon^{1/\bar\beta}$
  for fixed reference values $\bar\alpha \in (0,2)$, $\bar\beta \in (0,1)$.

**Conjecture C1 (Sufficiency).** If $\alpha \in C^0(\mathbb{R})$ and $\beta \in C^0(\mathbb{R})$,
then $X^\epsilon \Rightarrow X$ weakly in $D([0,T], \mathbb{R})$ as $\epsilon \to 0$, where $X$ is
a well-defined stochastic process whose one-dimensional distributions have density $u(x,t)$
satisfying (in a distributional sense) the variable-order fractional diffusion equation
$$\partial_t^{\beta(x)} u(x,t) = -(-\Delta_x)^{\alpha(x)/2}\, u(x,t).$$

**Conjecture C1 (Necessity).** If $\alpha$ has a jump discontinuity at some $x_0 \in \mathbb{R}$
(i.e., $\lim_{x \nearrow x_0} \alpha(x) \neq \lim_{x \searrow x_0} \alpha(x)$), then $\{X^\epsilon\}$
does not converge weakly in $D([0,T], \mathbb{R})$: there exist two sequences of CTRWs,
both satisfying the above conditions with the given $\alpha$, whose rescaled limits are distinct
(or do not exist).

## Special Cases

| Case | What it gives | Status |
|------|--------------|--------|
| $\alpha(x) = \alpha_0$, $\beta(x) = \beta_0$ constant | Classical Meerschaert-Scheffler CTRW limit theorem | **Known** ✓ |
| $\alpha$ piecewise constant, one jump at $x_0$ | Scaling limit ambiguous at interface — key counterexample | **Target for C1-necessity** |
| $\alpha \in \text{Lip}(\mathbb{R})$ (Lipschitz) | Local parameter variation $O(\epsilon^{1/\alpha(x)}) \to 0$ in scaling limit | Expected to converge; weaker than $C^1$ |
| $\alpha \in C^1(\mathbb{R})$ | Operator well-defined in variable-order pseudo-differential calculus (Samko) | **Strongest case — PDE interpretation cleanest** |

## Stepping-Stone Hierarchy

**S1 (weakest — target first):**  
For $\alpha(x) = \alpha_1 \mathbf{1}_{x < 0} + \alpha_2 \mathbf{1}_{x \geq 0}$ with $\alpha_1 \neq \alpha_2$,
the rescaled CTRW does NOT converge weakly in $D([0,T], \mathbb{R})$.

This establishes necessity (discontinuity breaks convergence) without needing the full sufficiency result.

**S2:**  
For $\alpha \in C^0(\mathbb{R})$ (continuous, bounded away from 0 and 2),
$X^\epsilon \Rightarrow X$ weakly.

This establishes sufficiency at the weakest regularity that makes sense.

**S3 (full conjecture):**  
Necessity and sufficiency together: continuity of $\alpha, \beta$ is the sharp threshold.

**S4 (beyond C1 — open):**  
Determine whether $C^0$ is truly sharp or whether Hölder continuity $C^\gamma$ for some
$\gamma > 0$ is the true threshold. (Conjecture: $C^0$ is sufficient but the proof may
naturally give Hölder.)

---

## Initial Proof Angles

**For S1 (necessity via counterexample):**  
Two CTRW approximations to the step-function $\alpha$: one uses a smooth mollification
$\alpha_\delta(x) \to \alpha_{\text{step}}$ from the left, another from the right. The two
limits as $\epsilon \to 0$ then $\delta \to 0$ will differ because the scaling near $x_0$
is different. Make this explicit via the Fourier transform of the transition density near $x_0$.

**For S2 (sufficiency):**  
The standard approach is: (i) tightness of $\{X^\epsilon\}$ via Aldous criterion, then
(ii) characterisation of limit points via the generator of the limiting semigroup.

Tightness: likely holds even for discontinuous $\alpha$ (the process is bounded in probability).
The continuity condition is needed for step (ii) — the generator of the limiting process is
$-(-\Delta)^{\alpha(x)/2}$, which is only well-defined as a pseudo-differential operator when
$\alpha \in C^0$ (Samko 1995; Umarov-Steinberg 2009).

**Key references to read:**
- Meerschaert & Scheffler (2004), "Limit theorems for continuous-time random walks"
- Samko (1995), "Fractional integration and differentiation of variable order"
- Umarov & Steinberg (2009), "Variable order differential equations with piecewise constant order-function"
- Chechkin, Gorenflo & Sokolov (2005), "Fractional diffusion in inhomogeneous media"
- Fedotov & Falconer (2012), "Subdiffusive master equation with space-dependent anomalous exponent"
- Angstman et al. (relevant CTRW papers — researcher to supply)

## Open Questions

- Is the sharp threshold $C^0$, or Hölder $C^\gamma$?
- Do $\alpha$ and $\beta$ have the same regularity requirement, or can one be rougher than the other?
- On a bounded domain, does the boundary condition interact with the regularity of $\alpha$ near the boundary?
- Is the limiting PDE $\partial_t^{\beta(x)} u = -(-\Delta)^{\alpha(x)/2} u$ the right operator ordering,
  or should it be $-(-\Delta_x)^{\alpha(x)/2} u$ interpreted symmetrically? (Operator ordering matters
  for non-constant $\alpha$.)
